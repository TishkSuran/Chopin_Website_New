BIOGRAPHY

Frédéric Chopin

A brilliant Romantic pianist who changed 19th century music and still inspires listeners today.

The Life of a Musical Genius

Frédéric Chopin (1810-1849) was a Polish composer and pianist who wrote mainly for solo piano. His unique musical style and technical skill made him one of the most important composers of his time.

Source: Wikipedia's Frédéric Chopin article.

Timeline of Chopin's Life

1810

Birth and Childhood

Born March 1, 1810 near Warsaw. His father was French, his mother Polish. The family moved to Warsaw shortly after his birth.

Showed musical talent early, performing concerts by age seven.

1816-1830

Education

Studied piano with Wojciech Żywny (1816-1821) and later at the Warsaw Conservatory under Józef Elsner.

Performed successfully in Warsaw and made his Vienna debut in 1829.

1830-1831

Leaving Poland

Left Warsaw for Vienna in November 1830. After the failed Polish uprising against Russia, he couldn't return home.

Became part of the Polish exile community in Europe.

1831-1838

Paris Years

Settled in Paris in 1831 and befriended famous artists like Liszt, Berlioz, and Delacroix.

Made a living teaching piano and publishing music. Preferred small private performances to public concerts.

Met writer George Sand in 1836 and began a relationship with her in 1838.

1838-1847

With George Sand

Spent winter 1838-39 in Majorca for his health. Composed his 24 Preludes during this time.

Summered at Sand's estate in Nohant (1839-1846), writing many important works.

Their relationship ended in 1847 due to family conflicts.

1842-1849

Final Years

Health declined from 1842 onward. Gave his last Paris concert in 1848.

Briefly visited London and Scotland before returning to Paris in late 1848.

Died October 17, 1849 at age 39. His funeral drew 3,000 people.

"Bach is an astronomer, discovering the most marvelous stars. Beethoven challenges the universe. I only try to express the soul and the heart of man."
— Frédéric Chopin

Legacy

Chopin became a symbol of the Romantic era through his music, his Polish heritage, his relationship with George Sand, and his early death.

His works remain essential for pianists worldwide. The International Chopin Piano Competition, started in 1927, continues his legacy.

His influence extends beyond music to literature, film, and culture, making him one of history's most beloved composers.
